Planetary magnetism, interiors,
and space environment

University of California, Los Angeles

Magnetic fields and gravity are how we see inside a planet.

The group studies how those signals reveal the otherwise hidden interiors, deep fluid motion, and space environments of planets and moons — connecting spacecraft observations with geophysical theory, numerical modeling, and magnetometer development, so the same measurements illuminate both planetary structure and the environments spacecraft travel through.

Research Areas

Across giant planets, ocean worlds, the Moon, and Earth's space environment, the group uses physical signals measured by spacecraft and instruments to infer what cannot be observed directly.

About Hao Cao

Associate Professor, Department of Earth, Planetary, and Space Sciences, University of California, Los Angeles.

Dr. Hao Cao is an Associate Professor of Space Science at UCLA. His research treats magnetic fields and gravity as windows into the hidden interiors and surrounding space environments of planetary bodies. The group works across observation, theory, modeling, and magnetometer development, with applications ranging from giant planets and icy moons to the Moon and Earth's near-space environment.

He is a member of the Juno Science Team, a Co-Investigator on ESA's JUICE J-MAG instrument, Deputy Instrument Lead for NASA's TRACERS magnetometer, and Principal Investigator of the L-MAG lunar magnetometer. He earned his Ph.D. at UCLA and held positions at Caltech and Harvard before returning.
